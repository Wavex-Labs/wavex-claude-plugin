What it does

Tell it who you are writing to and what you want from them. It writes a first email and two follow-ups, one for day 3 and one for day 7, each under 120 words. The first email is saved as a draft in your Gmail. Nothing is sent and nothing is scheduled: you send each email yourself.

You provide: who the person is, what you want from them, and (optional) who you are and their email address.

You get: three emails with subject lines, one line on what each email is doing, and a Gmail draft of the first one.

Limits: it uses only the facts you give. It does not look anyone up, and it never makes up praise, results, client names or numbers. One person per run. It is for one-to-one outreach, not bulk mail. It only creates a draft: it does not read your inbox.

Sample output

Email 1, send today

Subject: Day-after plans for wedding guests

Hi Maria,

You said in a trade newsletter that your wedding guests keep asking what to do the day after. With about 40 weddings a year at Example Harbor Hotel, I imagine it comes up often.

I'm Alex at Example Cove Charters. We run private half-day boat trips for groups of up to 12, which could be a simple answer to give those guests.

Would you be open to a 15-minute call next week to see if it fits?

Alex
Example Cove Charters

Email 2, send on day 3 if no reply

Subject: Re: Day-after plans for wedding guests

Hi Maria,

One detail I left out: each trip is private and takes up to 12 people. A couple could offer it to close family or the wedding party and keep the boat to themselves.

If that could help with the day-after question, would a 15-minute call work for you?

Alex
Example Cove Charters

Email 3, send on day 7 if no reply

Subject: Re: Day-after plans for wedding guests

Hi Maria,

This is my last note on this. If day-after boat trips are not a fit for your guests, there is no need to reply.

If it is worth a look later, a 15-minute call is all it takes, and I would be glad to hear from you.

Alex
Example Cove Charters

Why these are written this way

  • Email 1 opens with her own words about the day after, so it is about her guests and not about the sender.
  • Email 2 adds one new fact, that each trip is private for up to 12 people, and asks again in one line.
  • Email 3 says it is the last note and makes "no" an easy answer.
